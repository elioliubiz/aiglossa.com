Matching music to your cut: how to pick the length

Updated 23 October 2026 · 4 min read

The length of a track is a production decision, not a musical one. Pick it from the edit, not from the genre — and most editing problems disappear before they start.

Start from the cut, not the track

Before choosing a tempo or a mood, answer one question: how long is the piece of video the music sits under?

  • Is it the whole video, or one section?
  • Does it end on a hard cut, or fade to black?
  • Is there a voice-over that needs room at the start?

The answer tells you the duration. Everything else is secondary.

A rough map

5–15 sIdents, stings, logo reveals. Usually you want no intro at all.
30 sSocial clips, product teasers. The most common length we see.
45–60 sExplainers, ads, podcast intros. Enough room for a phrase to resolve.
75–90 sNarrative beats, menu loops, longer intros.

Leave room for the fade

A 30-second track under a 30-second cut has nowhere to fade. Either the music stops abruptly or it fights the picture.

Two habits that fix this:

  • Ask for the exact length and let the tail fade inside the last second;
  • Or cut the music 1–2 seconds before the picture ends.

What you should never do is stretch a 28-second track to 30 — the pitch drifts and the rhythm drags.

If the edit is longer than 90 seconds

Generate a loop instead of a longer track. A seamless loop repeats without a seam, so a 30-second loop can carry a three-minute scene. This is standard practice for menus, ambient beds and interactive scenes, and it costs far less than generating minutes of unique music.

The catch: loops work best when the scene length divides evenly by the loop length. 30 s into 90 s works; 30 s into 100 s does not — the seam lands mid-phrase and you hear it.

What about cuts that change length?

Every editor knows the feeling: the picture is locked, then it is not. Two ways to stay ahead:

  • Generate one length longer than you think you need, and trim the tail yourself;
  • Or generate the loop version first, and only commit to an exact length once the cut is locked.

Silence is a length too

Not every second needs music. A track that starts two seconds in, after a line of dialogue, often works better than one that starts at 0.000 — and it is easier to make, because the start no longer has to land on a beat.
